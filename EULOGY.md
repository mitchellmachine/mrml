# In Memory of Terry A. Davis

Terry A. Davis was an American programmer best known as the creator of
TempleOS, an extraordinary individual engineering project he developed largely
by himself. TempleOS brought together its own kernel and operating environment,
HolyC language and compiler, graphics, filesystem, shell and development
environment, applications, and tools. In a 2014 interview, Davis described
working inside TempleOS with his own compiler, editor, and tools.[^uses-this]

MRML acknowledges Davis because TempleOS demonstrates that one person can
reason about and construct an unusually large portion of a computer as a
coherent whole. The appreciation is for the programmer and the scale and unity
of the work—not for every technical or personal decision associated with it,
and not as a claim that MRML copies TempleOS.

MRML deliberately takes a different architectural path: memory-safe ordinary
applications, capability security, a small microkernel, isolated userspace
services, VM compartmentalization, modern hardware protection, networking,
trusted GUI composition, and AI operating through explicit authority. The
lesson carried forward is that an ambitious computer can remain intentionally
designed and understandable end to end without discarding modern defensive
boundaries. That principle is made concrete in the normative
[MRML Engineering Constitution](docs/ENGINEERING_CONSTITUTION.md).

## A life and its difficulties

Davis's technical accomplishment existed alongside profound personal hardship.
He lived with serious psychiatric illness, and later experienced periods of
homelessness. Those circumstances should be described compassionately, not
romanticized, sensationalized, or treated as the source of his ability. His
suffering was not a prerequisite for creativity.

Davis died on August 11, 2018, after being struck by a Union Pacific train in
The Dalles, Oregon. Contemporary local reporting confirmed the death and his
homelessness.[^chronicle] Available reporting says investigators did not reach a
conclusive determination between accident and suicide.[^biography] This project
does not speculate beyond that uncertainty.

## Final video

[Terry A. Davis — Final Video, August 11, 2018](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oH41gGBVpkE)

The video was uploaded only hours before his death.[^biography] In it Davis
reflects on having removed much of his online material, speaks about
purification, and considers his own situation and identity. This repository
links to the video but does not reproduce or redistribute it or its transcript.

## Purpose of this memorial

This file preserves an honest acknowledgement, not mythology. It does not call
Davis a prophet, make claims about rankings among programmers, excuse harmful
public conduct, or suggest that psychiatric illness creates genius. TempleOS is
appropriately remembered here as an extraordinary individual engineering
achievement. MRML preserves its own independent identity and chooses modern
isolation, validation, capability control, and evidence-driven engineering.
